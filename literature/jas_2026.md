## Semantics (published)

**Place-timed** PN for resource-allocation systems (Definition 1): each
activity place `p ∈ P_A` has a deterministic operation time `D(p)`; a token in
an activity place is *available* only when its remaining time is 0; time
elapses by decreasing token remaining times; transitions fire instantaneously
when all input activity places have available tokens (resource places are
ordinary input/output places with weights). State `S = (M,R)` includes the
token-remaining-time matrix `R`. This is **not** a transition-timed TPN.

## Manufacturing system of Figs. 5-6

Robotic cellular manufacturing system, adapted from Huang et al. (TASE 2022,
JAS ref. [23]) and Chen & Li (Automatica 2011, [24]), Huang et al. (TSMC 2015,
[25]). Three robots R1-R3, machines M1,M2 (2 copies each), M3,M4 (3 copies
each; the basic data file uses 2 for M3/M4 and 1 for M1... verified against
the file), loading I1-I3 and unloading O1-O3 buffers, job types J1-J3 with
routes and deterministic operation times (e.g. J1: I1→R1(3)→M1(2)→R2(6)→
M2(2)→R3(4)→O1 or the alternative route via M3/M4).

The corresponding PN (Fig. 6) is a 20-transition place-timed net; the author
data `ChenFig6_111` is 29 places × 20 transitions and reproduces the initial
marking, operation times and routes exactly. **Recorded inconsistency:** the
JAS text says "26 places" while the figure labels p1..p29 and the data has 29
columns; we use the data and report the discrepancy.

## Usability for our case

The J1 route of Fig. 6 is a realistic published manufacturing subnet with
robot/transport and machine resources, shared resources and an alternative
route; the operation times are published. It is our source for the adapted
21-place/16-transition candidate (`manufacturing/ADAPTATION.md`), with
place-timed → strong-time conversion and clearly labelled extensions (labels,
inspection/rework, one unobservable fault). It has no native faults or
observations, so it cannot be a "direct published example".
